# Legal Routing for Unwanted Email

Read this reference only when a task requires a legal claim, direct-marketing objection, deletion or access request, regulator complaint, or jurisdiction choice. It is operational guidance, not a substitute for advice from a qualified lawyer.

## Classify before citing law

Establish these facts from evidence, not assumptions:

1. **Message type:** direct marketing, mixed marketing/service content, purely transactional or security notice, political or charitable promotion, phishing, fraud, or malware.
2. **Recipient:** individual consumer, sole trader, employee address, generic business address, or legal entity.
3. **Jurisdictions:** recipient location, apparent sender or controller location, establishment targeting the recipient, and regulator competence.
4. **Relationship and consent:** requested message, express consent, customer relationship or soft opt-in, prior objection, and any evidence of withdrawal.
5. **Desired result:** unsubscribe, objection to marketing, suppression, erasure, source/access information, infrastructure enforcement, or regulator investigation.

Do not label ordinary account, delivery, security, contractual, or legally required notices as marketing merely because they are unwanted. A service message containing significant promotion may be mixed marketing; verify the applicable official guidance.

## Legal safety rules

- Check the current statutory text and regulator guidance immediately before relying on them. Laws, guidance, forms, deadlines, and regulator competence change.
- Use the rule that applies to the recipient and sender; do not combine the strongest provisions from different jurisdictions.
- Separate an enforceable right from good practice, a regulator's discretion, and an infrastructure provider's acceptable-use policy.
- State only facts supported by the message, authenticated headers, consent history, and prior correspondence. Use conditional wording for unresolved identity or jurisdiction.
- Do not threaten penalties, litigation, criminal liability, or publicity. Do not quote maximum fines as if the user will receive them.
- Minimize disclosures. Before submitting a complaint, identify required personal data, evidence, publication rules, and whether the complainant may become a witness.
- Prefer the narrowest effective request. Erasure is not automatic when legal retention or suppression is necessary; a suppression record may be needed to honor an objection.

## EU and EEA

- GDPR Article 21(2) gives a data subject the right to object at any time to processing for direct marketing; Article 21(3) requires that the personal data then no longer be processed for those purposes. Verify against the current [official GDPR text on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).
- Electronic-mail marketing is also governed by national law implementing Article 13 of the ePrivacy Directive. Consent and customer-relationship exceptions are channel-specific; verify the recipient country's implementation through an official legal source.
- Use GDPR access, source, recipient, or erasure rights only when they serve the user's requested outcome. Do not bundle them automatically with every marketing objection.
- Route a GDPR complaint to the competent data-protection authority only after checking its current jurisdiction, required prior contact, evidence, and form.

### Germany

- Section 7 UWG generally treats advertising by electronic mail without prior express consent as unacceptable harassment, subject to the cumulative existing-customer exception in section 7(3). Check the current [official text of § 7 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html).
- A marketing objection can rely on GDPR Article 21(2) and (3) when personal data are processed for direct marketing. Keep it distinct from the UWG question of whether sending the message was permissible.
- The Bundesnetzagentur's email-spam route is limited. Its published route concerns unsolicited advertising email or fax containing a promoted telephone number. Verify eligibility and current instructions on the [official Bundesnetzagentur page](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/TK/Aerger/Faelle/FaxMailSpam/start.html).
- For phishing or fraud, do not use a marketing objection or contact details from the message. Prefer verified infrastructure-abuse channels and, where appropriate, official police or cybercrime reporting routes.

## United Kingdom

- Determine whether the recipient is an individual subscriber or a corporate subscriber and whether the message is direct marketing, a service message, or mixed content.
- Verify consent, soft-opt-in, identification, and objection rules through the ICO's current [electronic-mail marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/). UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act may also apply when personal data are used.
- Check current ICO complaint prerequisites and forms before transmitting evidence or personal data.

## United States

- CAN-SPAM applies to commercial email and addresses header accuracy, deceptive subjects, advertising identification, a postal address, an opt-out method, and honoring opt-outs. Verify current requirements and deadlines in the [FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).
- Do not imply that CAN-SPAM requires prior consent in every case or that an individual necessarily has a private federal claim. Use the current FTC or other competent authority route.
- State and sector-specific privacy or consumer-protection rules may add obligations; verify them from official sources when material.

## Canada

- CASL commonly requires consent, sender identification, and a functioning unsubscribe mechanism for commercial electronic messages. Verify scope, implied-consent exceptions, and the current complaint route through the [CRTC CASL guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).
- The CRTC states that unsubscribe requests must be processed without delay and no later than 10 business days; recheck the current rule before citing the deadline.

## Australia

- The Spam Act framework generally requires consent, sender identification, and a usable unsubscribe facility for commercial electronic messages. Verify the current rules and complaint route through [ACMA's spam guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam).
- ACMA guidance states that unsubscribe requests are generally actioned within five working days; recheck before citing the deadline.

## Drafting contract

A legal message or complaint should contain only:

1. the verified recipient and destination;
2. the relevant message date, subject, authenticated source, and relationship or consent facts;
3. the narrow requested outcome;
4. one verified legal basis when it materially helps;
5. minimum required evidence and personal data; and
6. the no-routine-reply closing from [remediation.md](remediation.md) when appropriate.

If jurisdiction, sender identity, consent, or message classification remains uncertain, say so and avoid a definitive allegation. Escalate high-stakes, disputed, or litigation-oriented questions to a qualified professional.
